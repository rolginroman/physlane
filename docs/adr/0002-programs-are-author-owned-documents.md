# Programs are author-owned documents; sharing grants views, never mutation

A program belongs to its author: sharing (public page, share link, later purchase) grants read
access and nothing more. Trainees act only on forks — snapshot copies with attribution that
never sync. Runs (phase 2) attach to owned or forked programs, hold a point-in-time snapshot,
and denormalize program content into workout logs so history survives author edits and
deletion. Author edits apply live to the original only; deletion soft-hides behind a "removed"
page, with hard purge reserved for moderation. Paid programs later disable forking — this same
boundary is the paywall's enforcement mechanism.

## Considered Options

Linked forks with upstream sync — rejected: merge machinery that fights trainee customization
(forking exists so people can adjust), and the worst possible author economics once paid access
ships ("buy once, get every future update free").

## Amendment (2026-10-25, plan review)

"This same boundary is the paywall's enforcement mechanism" was overbroad: disabling forks
prevents copying but gates nothing — public and unlisted views still render full content.
Paid access (Phase 3) will need a fourth visibility class (access-gated), and runs may need to
attach through access grants rather than forks. Deferred to Phase 3 kickoff; nothing in Phase
1 may assume runs require forks.
