Protein supports muscle repair, satiety, and stable energy across the day. The internet argument is rarely about whether you need protein. It is about whether each meal needs a magic number or whether the daily total is enough.

Older guidance suggested a hard cap per meal for muscle protein synthesis.[^1] Newer work is more forgiving: larger meals still contribute, especially when total daily protein is adequate and you train consistently.[^2]

## Per meal vs per day

For most people, hitting a sensible daily range matters more than splitting grams with a calculator. Two or three substantial protein feeds plus snacks can work; so can four smaller meals if that fits your schedule.

Distribution helps when it makes adherence easier, not because missing thirty grams in one sitting zeros out the meal. Think in terms of meals you will actually repeat on a busy Tuesday.

## Practical targets

Aim for a protein source at each main meal, then adjust portions until weekly averages land in your target band. Lean meats, dairy, eggs, legumes, and supplemental powder all count toward the same total.[^3]

If you are gaining or maintaining muscle while losing fat, slightly higher protein often makes the process feel smoother. Tune the number to appetite, digestion, and preference rather than to forum folklore.
