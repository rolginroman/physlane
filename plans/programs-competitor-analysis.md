# Competitor analysis: shareable and sellable training programs

**Date:** 2026-10-25. Prices are in USD unless noted. Each price shows where it came from: **[official, fetched 2026-10-25]** means I read the vendor's own page today. **[3P]** means a third-party review or aggregator. Treat [3P] figures as approximate. **Unverified** means I could not confirm the point from any source.

---

## TL;DR

1. **Free "any user writes and shares a program" is already commoditized.**
   - Boostcamp ships almost exactly our MVP for free. Its builder runs up to 18 weeks and supports RPE or %1RM plus per-exercise instructions. Users publish to a "By users" library or share a link, and copy each other's programs. It has 14,000+ community programs.
   - Hevy and Liftosaur both share programs by link.
   - The free-sharing tier cannot be our differentiator. ([Boostcamp builder](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/program-builder), [Boostcamp Pro](https://boostcamp.app/pro), [Hevy sharing](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/share-folders-routines/))
2. **Per-program monthly access with a platform fee already exists.** TrainHeroic Marketplace is the closest match to our phase 2. Coaches set a monthly "team" subscription price and choose a public or private listing. TrainHeroic also has a marketplace for platform-driven discovery. TrainingPeaks (endurance) sells one-time plans at a 70/30 split and supports private URLs, which work like our unlisted links. ([TrainHeroic sell](https://www.trainheroic.com/?p=239572), [TrainingPeaks help](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/204072494))
3. **Nobody in the set ties program adherence to measured energy expenditure or bodyweight trend.** That gap is physlane's real wedge. MacroFactor is the threat to watch. It has the adaptive-TDEE app plus "MacroFactor Workouts" (launched January 2026) with Jeff Nippard programs and videos, and the two are sold as a bundle. It has no creator marketplace that I could find. ([MacroFactor Workouts](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/), [MF Dec 2025](https://macrofactorapp.com/mm-dec-2025/))
4. **Coaching on logged workouts is sold as per-client seat SaaS that the coach pays for.** Examples are TrueCoach, Trainerize, Everfit, CoachRx and Hevy Coach. On the consumer side it is a premium 1:1 tier at $99–$200/month (Future, Trainwell, Caliber). No product in the set lets a trainee pay a creator monthly for a program and get coaching on their logs in the same product, on a marketplace, at low cost. TrainHeroic's team subscriptions come closest.

## Per-product findings

### Consumer marketplaces and creator apps

**Playbook**
- **Authors:** Fitness creators who apply. Onboarding has historically favored an existing audience and a content library ([TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/17/playbook-a-creator-platform-focused-on-fitness-raises-3-million-in-seed)). A competitor says approval is required ([Exercise.com](https://www.exercise.com/grow/how-much-does-playbook-cost)), so treat that with caution.
- **Sharing:** Each creator gets a branded subscription experience with community and challenges ([llms.txt](https://www.playbookapp.io/llms.txt)). I found no public cross-creator browse marketplace in the official copy, so that point is unverified.
- **Monetization:** Creators set prices for subscriptions and programs. The standard split is **80% creator / 20% Playbook of Net Revenue**. Net Revenue means after processing fees, app-store fees, refunds and taxes ([Creator Agreement, effective 2026-05-12](https://joinplaybook.com/creator-agreement)). Because app-store fees are taken first, the creator's effective share is lower ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/playbook/)). Playbook says it has paid out $233M+ to 2,500+ earning creators ([llms.txt](https://www.playbookapp.io/llms.txt)).
- **Copy policy:** The creator keeps ownership. Playbook gets a non-exclusive, sublicensable license to host and promote the content, and that license survives termination as needed for existing users ([Creator Agreement](https://joinplaybook.com/creator-agreement)).
- **Coaching:** Community and challenges. I did not verify 1:1 messaging.
- **Good / bad:** Proven payout scale. Mobile IAP gives up a large cut. The model is a creator storefront, not a discovery market.

**Boostcamp** (the closest match to our MVP)
- **Authors:** Two tiers:
  - 130+ curated coach programs from names such as Eric Helms, Jim Wendler and GZCL/Cody Lefever. Most are free; 20+ are Pro-only.
  - 14,000+ community programs written by any user ([features](https://boostcamp.app/features), [Pro](https://boostcamp.app/pro)).
- **Sharing:** Users publish to the "By users" library or share a link. Published programs appear in followers' feeds with a "Save Program" button ([builder](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/program-builder), [community](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/community)). I could not confirm whether a shared link stays out of the public library (true unlisted).
- **Structure:**
  - Up to 18 weeks and 7 days per week.
  - Two week modes: repeating weeks or periodized weeks.
  - Set targets: reps, rep range, rep max, AMRAP, time.
  - Intensity: RPE, RPE range, or %1RM.
  - Supersets and phase titles.
  - Per-exercise instructions "for whoever runs the program" ([builder](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/program-builder)).
- **Versioning:** Edits apply to new savers. Existing savers keep the version they started ([builder](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/program-builder)).
- **Exercise DB:** Demos for each exercise ([features](https://boostcamp.app/features)). I did not find the library size. Free users get 3 custom exercises; Pro gets unlimited.
- **Monetization:** Free core. Pro costs $59.99/year or $14.99/month **[official, fetched 2026-10-25]** ([Pro](https://boostcamp.app/pro)). I found **no payouts to community authors**. My search for creator monetization returned nothing relevant.
- **Coaching:** Social feed with comments and reactions. No coach-client channel.
- **Copy policy:** Copying shared community programs is a free, featured action ([features](https://boostcamp.app/features)).
- **Trust risk:** A community program titled "Jeff Nippard's Fundamentals" is published by an unrelated user ([page](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/LbRcvP-jeff-nippards-fundamentals)). Nippard sells "Fundamentals" for $39.99 ([jeffnippard.com](https://jeffnippard.com/product/fundamentals-hypertrophy-program)). Any-user authoring invites knock-offs of paid programs.

**Ladder**
- **Authors:** Staff coaches only. There are 23 coach-led "teams" (programs), and a quiz matches each member to one team. A new weekly plan comes from each team's coach ([llms.txt](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt)).
- **Sharing:** None. Members can switch team 2×/month on monthly PRO; switching is unlimited on annual.
- **Structure:** Weekly drops with audio coaching, video demos and pacing. Programs are for the whole team, not personalized.
- **Monetization:** Platform subscription. PRO is $29.99/month or $179.99/year **[official pricing page per 3P summary]** ([pricing](https://www.joinladder.com/pricing)). IAP-only PRO+/ELITE tiers cost $34.99–$49.99/month ([llms.txt](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt)).
- **Coaching:** Team Chat on all plans. 1:1 Coach Chat and form checks on ELITE ([llms.txt](https://www.joinladder.com/llms.txt)).
- **Good:** A curated, opinionated catalog of about 23 programs beats choice overload.

**Caliber**
- **Authors:** Caliber's staff coaches.
- **Tiers:**
  - Free: unlimited logging and 600+ exercises with demo videos ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/EG/app/id1482405410)).
  - Plus: coach-designed plans, about $12/month [3P] ([GGR](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/?p=171911)).
  - Group coaching ("Pro"): about $19/month [3P, unverified name] ([Trainwell comparison](https://www.trainwell.net/blog/trainwell-vs-caliber-personal-coaching-apps-compared-2026)).
  - Premium 1:1: from about $200/month [3P] ([stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/caliber)).
- **Coaching:** On Premium, in-app chat, video messaging, video form reviews and weekly progress reviews ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/EG/app/id1482405410)).
- **Sharing:** Not a marketplace.

**Future**
- **Authors:** An assigned 1:1 coach writes custom weekly programs ([future.co](https://www.future.co/)).
- **Price:** $50 for the first month, then **$199/month** **[official, fetched 2026-10-25]**.
- **Coaching:** Check-ins, accountability, and an exercise library with video demos and voice cues.
- **Sharing:** None.

**CoPilot (now Trainwell)**
- **Model:** Remote 1:1 coaching with unlimited text and video messaging.
- **Price:** $99/month is the most cited figure. A Trainwell landing page also shows $179/month billed quarterly [3P/official landing page, conflicting] ([Trainwell](https://www.trainwell.net/trainer), [GGR](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/trainwell-fitness-review)).
- **Sharing:** None.

**Centr**
- **Authors:** Staff and celebrity-trainer content. Platform subscription of about $29.99/month or $119.99–$159.99/year [3P, verified 2026-07] ([recurdash](https://recurdash.com/subscription-pricing/centr)).
- **Recent direction:** centr.com now leads with home-gym hardware such as a $3,299 functional trainer ([centr.com](https://centr.com/)).
- **Sharing / coaching:** No user authoring. No coaching found.

### Logging apps with program sharing

**Hevy**
- **Authors:** Any user.
- **Sharing:** A routine or folder shares as a link to a public hevy.com page, for example [hevy.com/routine/…](https://hevy.com/routine/GPmZyr7r4Em). Recipients can save a copy. Users can also copy routines from another user's completed workout ([features](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/share-folders-routines/)).
- **Pricing:** Hevy Pro is $2.99/month, $23.99/year or $74.99 lifetime [App Store via 3P].
- **Hevy Coach:** A separate SaaS starting at $25 (official pricing page) with seat brackets from 1–10 up to 501–1,000 clients. It includes client chat, and **clients get Hevy Pro free** ([pricing](https://hevycoach.com/pricing/)).
- **Copy policy:** Copying is free and intended.
- **Monetization of programs:** None.

**Strong**
- **Sharing:** A share sheet sends routines and workouts to friends ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/pk/app/id464254577)). I did not verify a public link format.
- **Pricing:** Free tier allows 3 custom routines. PRO is $4.99/month or $29.99/year.
- **Program monetization / marketplace:** None.

**StrengthLog**
- **Authors:** StrengthLog staff. Basic programs are free; advanced ones need premium (14-day trial) ([programs](https://www.strengthlog.com/training-programs/)).
- **Pricing:** Premium is $16.90/month, $37.90/quarter or $109/year ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/apple-store/id1434229662)).
- **Sharing:** The App Store subscriber-benefits list includes "sharing workouts and programs with other users." It is unclear whether that is premium-only.

**RP Hypertrophy**
- **Authors:** Renaissance Periodization staff. 45+ mesocycle templates plus a custom builder. The algorithm adjusts volume and load from pump, soreness and workload feedback ([RP](https://rpstrength.com/hypertrophy-app), [Boostcamp comparison](https://www.boostcamp.app/vs/rp-hypertrophy)).
- **Pricing:** No free tier. UK listing shows £34.99/month or £299.99/year; AU listing shows A$59.99/month or A$499.99/year ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1555614554)). I did not find the US price.
- **Sharing:** I found none.

**Liftosaur** (added)
- **Authors:** Any user writes programs in "Liftoscript," a text DSL.
- **Sharing:** Shared by link; recipients import into the app.
- **Monetization:** An **affiliate program** pays creators commission when their programs bring in users ([affiliates](https://www.liftosaur.com/affiliates)). I did not find the commission rate.

**MacroFactor Workouts** (added; adjacent to physlane's core)
- **Launch:** January 2026, from the adaptive-TDEE MacroFactor team.
- **Content:** Generated or custom programs, 900+ exercises with tips and cues, technique videos by Jeff Nippard, and import of selected Nippard routines.
- **Pricing:** $11.99/month standalone. Bundle with MacroFactor for $71.99 in the first year, then $89.99/year ([workouts](https://macrofactor.com/workouts/), [Dec 2025](https://macrofactorapp.com/mm-dec-2025/)).
- **Gaps:** I found no user sharing and no creator marketplace.

### Algorithmic apps (for contrast)

- **Fitbod:** Algorithm-generated sessions from your training history. $15.99/month or $95.99/year; 1,600+ exercises with video [3P, competitor-authored] ([SensAI](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/fitbod-review-2026)). No authoring market.
- **JuggernautAI:** AI powerlifting programming at $34.99/month or $349.99/year [3P, Oct 2026] ([SensAI](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/juggernaut-ai-vs-rp-hypertrophy-vs-alpha-progression-2026)). No sharing.
- **TrainerRoad:** Adaptive endurance plans with 3,000+ structured workouts, $21.99/month or $209.99/year [3P, Jul 2026] ([join.cc](https://join.cc/ride-smarter/trainerroad)). App-led coaching, not human coaching.
- **TrainingPeaks** (the real endurance analogue for *sold* plans):
  - Coaches publish plans with a price and a sample week.
  - The split is **70/30** on both the marketplace and private URLs.
  - Minimum price is $4.95 on the marketplace; any price on private URLs.
  - A plan needs at least 7 workouts.
  - Dynamic plans can sell only on private URLs.
  - Event plans are removed automatically after the race date.
  - Sources: [help](https://help.trainingpeaks.com/hc/en-us/articles/204072494), [coach guide](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/get-started-coach/).

### Free and open channels

- **Muscle & Strength:** Staff-authored. 1,000+ free workout plans and a 1,500+ exercise video library, supported by ads and its supplement store ([about](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/learn-more); fetch returned 403, so these figures come from the search snippet).
- **MuscleWiki:**
  - Free library of 1,700–1,900+ filmed exercises with an interactive body map ([about](https://musclewiki.com/about)). Premium adds tracking and custom workouts.

  - This is a candidate source for our catalog's muscle graphics.
- **free-exercise-db:** 800+ exercises in JSON with images, released under the Unlicense, with a JSON schema and muscle fields ([repo](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db)). Image provenance is **unverified**, so check it before using the images.
- **r/Fitness wiki and spreadsheet culture:**
  - Programs such as GZCLP, 5/3/1 and nSuns spread as free text and spreadsheets ([LiftVault 5/3/1](https://liftvault.com/programs/powerlifting/jim-wendlers-531-spreadsheet/), [GZCLP](https://fitnessvolt.com/rpe-training/programs/gzclp/)). ## Synthesis

### Patterns by segment

- **Consumer marketplaces and creator apps:**
  - Authorship is gated: staff (Ladder, Centr, Caliber) or approved creators (Playbook).
  - Payment is either a platform subscription or a creator storefront with an 80/20 split before app-store fees.
  - Discovery happens off-platform, on the creator's social channels.
  - Coaching is a premium upsell: $35–50/month (Ladder ELITE) or $99–200/month for 1:1 (Trainwell, Future, Caliber).
- **Coach SaaS:**
  - The coach pays per seat, from $9 to about $280/month.
  - Programs are assigned, not browsed.
  - Feedback rides on logged workouts through comments, video and messaging.
  - Marketplaces are lead-generation add-ons (Trainerize.me, Everfit beta).
  - TrainHeroic is the exception: an actual program and subscription marketplace with a 70/30 split on platform-driven sales.
- **Logging apps:**
  - Any-user authoring and link sharing are free and copy-friendly (Hevy, Boostcamp, Liftosaur).
  - Monetization comes from analytics Pro tiers, never from paying authors. Liftosaur's affiliate program is the one exception.
  - Boostcamp has turned the r/Fitness spreadsheet canon into structured content.
- **Spreadsheet and PDF meta-channel:**
  - One-time purchases of $30–100 [Nippard example only].
  - Zero copy protection, and piracy is visible.
  - Top creators are migrating into partner apps (Nippard into MacroFactor).

### Where our model is crowded versus novel

| Our component | Status | Closest competitor |
|---|---|---|
| Any-user structured authoring with RPE, %1RM and cues | **Crowded and free** | Boostcamp, Hevy, Liftosaur |
| Public profile plus unlisted link | **Partially crowded** | Boostcamp publish or link; Hevy public routine pages; TrainingPeaks private URLs; TrainHeroic public or private listing |
| Per-program **monthly** paid access, platform fee only, Connect-style | **Exists but thin** | TrainHeroic team subscriptions; Playbook (creator-level subscription, not per program) |
| Coaching on trainee logs inside the purchased program | **Exists in coach SaaS and TrainHeroic**; absent from consumer logging apps | TrueCoach exercise comments; Hevy Coach; TrainHeroic |
| Tying program outcomes to **measured TDEE and bodyweight trend** | **Novel in this set** | MacroFactor + Workouts bundle is the threat, but it has no creator, sharing or coaching layer |

**Direct competitor to our exact combined model:** **TrainHeroic Marketplace** is the nearest. It covers any approved coach, public or private listing, monthly subscription programs, a low fee on self-driven sales, and logging. It differs in three ways: authoring is coach-gated (marketplace access is enabled through support), it is athlete- and S&C-oriented, and it has no nutrition or energy-expenditure data. Boostcamp competes only on the free MVP.

### Gaps and opportunities

1. **Outcome-backed programs.** No marketplace shows what a program actually did to trainees. We could show aggregate, anonymized adherence, bodyweight trend and measured TDEE change across runs. That is a trust signal Playbook, Boostcamp and TrainHeroic cannot copy, and it reuses physlane's core.
2. **Fee posture.** TrainHeroic's $1 per subscriber-month on self-driven sales undercuts 20–30% revenue shares. A web-first Stripe Connect flow avoids the app-store cut that eats Playbook creators' 80%. Competing on a low fee is credible.
3. **IP and knock-off moderation is unsolved.** Boostcamp hosts a third party's "Jeff Nippard's Fundamentals." Once programs are paid, takedown and attribution flows are table stakes.
4. **Exercise catalog sourcing.** MuscleWiki's commercial API ($10–200/month, muscle diagrams, primary/secondary/tertiary muscles) or the Unlicense free-exercise-db removes build cost. Video stays linked out, which matches our design.
5. **Coaching at a mid price.** There is a hole between a $15/month app and a $99–200/month 1:1 coach. Async comments on logged sets inside a paid program, on Ladder-style "Team Chat" economics, sit in that gap.

## Opinionated takeaways

1. **Do not market the MVP as "share programs for free."** Boostcamp already does that with 14,000+ community programs and no cap. Frame the MVP as the program layer of physlane's energy data, and lead with "see what this program did to bodyweight and TDEE." **What would change this:** Boostcamp or Hevy adding nutrition or energy tracking.
2. **Unlisted links are an MVP must, but treat them as low-value table stakes.** Snapshot program versions on save, like Boostcamp, so edits never silently change a running trainee's plan. This also makes later paid-version entitlement clean.
3. **Build the catalog rather than author media.** Seed it from free-exercise-db (Unlicense; image provenance needs checking) or license MuscleWiki's API for body-map graphics. Keep custom exercises per author, and do not merge them into the global catalog without review.
4. **Add attribution and reporting at MVP.** Include a "report / claim this program" path and a "based on <program/author>" field on forks before money arrives. The Boostcamp/Nippard example shows the failure mode.
5. **Phase 2 pricing: monthly per program, web checkout, flat low platform fee.** Anchor against TrainHeroic ($1 per subscriber-month on self-driven sales; 30% only when the platform drives the sale) rather than Playbook's 20%. Consider a two-rate split: low on creator-referred sales, higher on marketplace-discovered sales. **What would change this:** creators demanding one-time purchases, which is the TrainingPeaks and Nippard norm. Support "one-time = lifetime of that version" as a cheap second SKU only if asked.
6. **Paid content cannot be forked.** Free public programs can be fork-friendly, which matches norms on Hevy and Boostcamp. Paid programs should be run-only, with no export of the template, like the coach-SaaS norm. Accept that determined users will transcribe programs, as the PDF piracy shows. Value comes from coaching and outcome data, not the template.
7. **Phase 3 coaching: comment threads on logged sets plus a per-program group chat first; 1:1 video later.** TrueCoach-style exercise comments and Ladder-style team chat are cheap. 1:1 video review is the $99–200 tier, which is a different business.
8. **Watch MacroFactor.** They have adaptive TDEE, workouts, and Nippard as a distribution partner. If they add creator programs, they become the direct competitor. Our hedge is the open creator layer and coaching-on-logs, which they have not signalled.
